# Page 1 — The Axis: From Exposure to Understanding

The dominant assumption in global evangelism is that more exposure produces more conversion. This assumption is mistaken, and the mistake is expensive.

The Christian message has already reached billions. Yet exposure to Christian *vocabulary* does not produce understanding of the biblical *Gospel*. A person can hear the name of Jesus, recognize a cross, repeat a verse, and respond emotionally — all without grasping the holiness of God, the gravity of sin, the necessity of Christ's death, the reality of his bodily resurrection, the meaning of grace, the call to repentance, or the shape of life under Christ's reign. Christian words absorbed into a non-Christian worldview produce a confident convert to the wrong religion.

So the controlling question is not *"How do we reach more people faster?"* It is:

> How do we proclaim and teach the Gospel so that, when people hear it, they understand its meaning, confront its claims, respond in repentance and faith, obey Christ, join his people, and pass the message on without distorting it?

This changes everything — method, metric, formation, security, and church. A distribution problem is solved with more channels and volume. A *comprehension* problem is solved with patience, context, relationship, and formation. Most evangelistic infrastructure is built for distribution. This approach is built for comprehension.

**The governing formula — the one sentence worth memorizing whole:**

> **The message is fixed. The bridge is local. The learning pathway is adaptive.**

The *fixed message* is the apostolic Gospel — never negotiated. The *local bridge* is the culturally meaningful entry point that helps a particular person begin to understand. The *adaptive pathway* is the structured process that carries a learner from experience into the biblical story, then into discovery, retelling, obedience, reflection, clarification, discipleship, and multiplication.

**What success is — and is not.** Success is not that people *hear* a Christian message. It is that people become disciples who can retell the biblical Gospel accurately, explain why the cross and resurrection are necessary, tell the true Gospel from its counterfeits, obey Scripture concretely, live in accountable community, disciple others in their own networks, and form local churches that continue without outside control.

**The four blunt field tests:** *Can they retell it? Will they obey it? Is their character changing? Can they pass it on?*

# Page 2 — The Method, and the Gospel It Carries

**The Gospel, in nine movements, told in order** (because the doctrines depend on what comes before them — the cross is unintelligible without holiness and sin; grace, without guilt; resurrection, without death):

> Creation → Fall → Promise → Christ → Cross → Resurrection → Grace, Faith & Repentance → New Life & Kingdom → New Creation.

Two rules for telling it: never give Christ without the holiness and sin that make the cross necessary; never end without the call to repent and trust Christ.

**The teaching method — a guided cycle, not a monologue and not unguided discovery:**

> ANCHOR → QUESTION → STORY → DISCOVER → RETELL → DO → REFLECT → TOGETHER → CLARIFY → DISCIPLE

Begin from a concrete, culturally real experience (the *anchor*); ask questions that surface what people already assume; tell the biblical story in order; guide discovery; have someone *retell* it; invite one bounded act of obedience, framed as a mirror that exposes the heart, never as a way to earn grace; reflect in community; then state the Gospel explicitly; and move toward baptism, church, and disciple-making. (This cycle is a disciplined, falsifiable proposal — coherent and cautious, not yet proven superior to simpler methods. The full plan says so plainly and sets a research agenda.)

**Retelling is the heart of it.** A person has not understood the Gospel merely by agreeing with Christian words. The most reliable test is to have them tell it back: Who is God here? What went wrong with humanity? Why was the cross necessary? What do grace, faith, and repentance mean? Retelling exposes what is remembered, omitted, distorted, and ready to be passed on — and it needs no literacy, schooling, or written exam.

**Three layers, so no one carries more than their role requires:**

- **Any believer** can tell the Gospel, hear it retold, run a simple Scripture discussion, and flag what is missing or confused to a mentor.
- **The trained facilitator** runs the full cycle, handles cultural anchors, keeps obedience diagnostic, and reports to a mentor.
- **The regional mentor** maps worldviews, forms leaders, audits health, and guards against drift.

The simplicity of the base is a simplicity of *form, not of substance*: fewer pages, never fewer protections. No group, however remote, operates without a reachable mentor.

**Built to multiply without dependency.** The aim is the four-self church — self-supporting, self-governing, self-propagating, self-theologizing. Outside money may fund one-time, catalytic things (translation, first training); it may never fund the recurring costs that, if cut, would stop the movement. The one-sentence test: *if the outside money stopped today, would the movement keep multiplying?*

# Page 3 — The Fatal Risks

This approach can fail. Naming the failures is part of guarding against them. These are the dangers that would sink the work — each is treated at length, with its safeguard, in the full plan.

**The Gospel hollowed out.** Counterfeits keep the words (*Jesus, grace, faith, Kingdom*) and quietly change the meanings — prosperity, moralism, legalism, politics, therapy, permissiveness, fear, institutionalism, syncretism. Every message is checked against three questions: Does it center on Christ and his finished work? Is salvation God's unearned gift received through faith? Does it call people to repentance and holiness? *Safeguard: explicit Gospel clarification every cycle; retelling scored against a doctrinal standard; trained facilitators under mentors.*

**Obedience turned into moralism.** The obedience step is the most easily misused part of the method. The moment "obey and reflect" becomes "obey to prove your faith" or "obey to make God act," it has failed. *Safeguard: it is framed as diagnostic, never salvific; where a facilitator cannot hold that framing, it is dropped rather than risked.*

**Shallow multiplication.** Unguided discovery among biblical novices drifts into error or settles into moralism. Speed without depth produces exactly the hollow, drifting movement this approach exists to prevent. *Safeguard: guided discovery plus explicit clarification; a competence gate before anyone facilitates alone; mentoring that is never optional.*

**Dependency.** Foreign money, celebrity leaders, and proprietary platforms can quietly become the thing the local work leans on. *Safeguard: the four-self architecture and the spark-vs-dependency test, with a dated withdrawal plan on every external injection.*

**Manipulation and the pressure of numbers.** Fear, crowd dynamics, urgency, and quotas manufacture decisions that bypass understanding. A volunteer pressed to produce numbers will force responses and mistake reaction for fruit. *Safeguard: an anti-manipulation floor; no quotas; the volunteer's worth measured by faithfulness, not output.*

**Danger to people in hostile places.** This approach is used in prisons, refugee camps, and regions of persecution. A written record of who believes and where they meet can cost lives. The governing rule:

> **Measure where you can, sample where you must, and go silent where measuring would endanger lives.**

And a hard limit, stated so no one mistakes it: *this approach teaches comprehensible proclamation, not clandestine security.* It gives enough caution to prevent recklessness, but operational security under persecution — surveillance, physical risk, armed actors — exceeds its scope. Workers there must seek specialized training, and when in doubt, record nothing.

**The cost of going.** Where volunteers are sent into danger, the truth is told plainly before any commitment: protection cannot be guaranteed, the cost can be freedom, family, or life itself, and refusing a dangerous mission is not cowardice. No hidden cost, no coerced courage, no false promise of rescue — and the absence of a guarantee never cancels the sender's duty of care.

---

*The whole stands or falls on one thing: faithfulness to the Gospel it proclaims. If it gains audience but loses truth, it fails. If it gains speed but loses discipleship, it fails. If it gains money but breeds dependency, it fails. If it gains influence but loses Christ at the center, it fails. The Gospel does not need to be made powerful; it is the power of God for salvation. The task is only to proclaim it faithfully, teach it wisely, guard it carefully, and pass it on without distortion.*
