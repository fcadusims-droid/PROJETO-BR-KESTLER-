# Proclaiming the Gospel So That It Is Understood

### A Strategic and Pedagogical Framework for Faithful, Cross-Cultural Gospel Comprehension, Discipleship, and Multiplication

**Prepared for:** Gospel Mundial
**Date:** May 31, 2026

---

## How to read this document

This plan is deliberately structured in two parts, because it serves two audiences whose needs conflict.

**Part One (Sections 1–9)** is the *strategic and theological manifesto*. It is written for leaders, trainers, and partners who need to understand and defend the whole approach. It is meant to be read once, in order, and returned to for orientation.

**Part Two (the Field Kit, Sections 10–25e)** is a set of *standalone, reproducible tools*, sorted into three layers by who needs them — any believer, the trained facilitator, the regional mentor — so that no one carries more than their role requires. Each one is designed to be photocopied, recorded as audio, or memorized on its own, without the rest of the document. An ordinary believer facilitating a group in a village, a prison, or a refugee camp should never need to carry Part One, and should need only the Layer 1 cards to begin.

A central tension runs through any plan like this: the strategy is sophisticated, but the people who will carry it out must be able to work simply. A plan that tries to be both the manifesto and the field manual in a single continuous text becomes long, repetitive, and too heavy for the very "ordinary believers" it hopes to equip. This plan keeps the depth but quarantines it. The thinking lives in Part One; the doing lives in Part Two.

A note on confidence. Parts of this plan rest on Scripture and settled missiological experience. Other parts — particularly the cognitive and pedagogical claims — are reasoned proposals that have not been rigorously field-tested. This document marks that difference openly rather than hiding it, and Section 9 sets a research agenda for the parts that remain uncertain. A plan that overclaims its own evidence base is itself a small form of the dishonesty it warns against.

A note on safety and measurement. This plan is used in safe places and in dangerous ones — including prisons, refugee camps, and regions of active persecution. Its goal never changes: faithful *comprehension*. But how that comprehension is verified must change with the danger, because a written record of who believes and where they meet can cost lives. So the plan operates in three modes, chosen by the risk of the context: **Tracking** (open settings — measure normally), **Sampling** (sensitive settings — record only anonymous, aggregate, non-identifying findings), and **Silence** (high-risk settings — record nothing measurable; rely on oral, in-person discernment). The rule that governs all three: **measure where you can, sample where you must, and go silent where measuring would endanger lives.** A second rule follows from it and is stated plainly so no one mistakes the plan's competence: this document teaches comprehensible proclamation, not clandestine security. It gives enough caution to prevent recklessness, but operational security under persecution — surveillance, physical risk, armed actors — exceeds its scope, and workers in those contexts should seek specialized training and, when in doubt, record nothing.

# PART ONE — THE STRATEGIC FRAMEWORK

# 1. The Problem This Plan Exists to Solve

The dominant assumption in global evangelism is that more exposure produces more conversion. This assumption is mistaken, and the mistake is expensive.

The Christian message has already reached billions through churches, media, missions, public events, and cultural inheritance. Yet exposure to Christian *vocabulary* does not produce understanding of the biblical *Gospel*. A person can hear the name of Jesus, recognize a cross, repeat a verse, and respond emotionally to an appeal — all without grasping the holiness of God, the gravity of sin, the necessity of Christ's substitutionary death, the reality of his bodily resurrection, the meaning of grace, the call to repentance, or the shape of life under Christ's reign.

So the controlling question of this plan is not *"How do we reach more people faster?"* It is:

> How do we proclaim and teach the Gospel so that, when people hear it, they understand its meaning, confront its claims, respond in repentance and faith, obey Christ, join his people, and pass the message on without distorting it?

This reframes the entire enterprise. A distribution problem is solved with more channels and more volume. A *comprehension* problem is solved with patience, context, relationship, and formation. These call for opposite instincts. Most evangelistic infrastructure is built for distribution. This plan is built for comprehension.

### The strategic shift

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Exposure | Understanding |
| Campaigns | Discipleship pathways |
| Emotional decisions | Repentant faith and obedience |
| Centralized platforms | Locally reproducible communities |
| Imported religious culture | Faithful contextualization |
| Professional-ministry dependency | Equipped ordinary believers |
| Metrics of audience size | Metrics of comprehension and fruit |
| Technology as engine | Technology as servant |

### The target outcome

Success is not that people *hear* a Christian message. It is that people become disciples who can retell the biblical Gospel accurately, explain why the cross and resurrection are necessary, distinguish the true Gospel from its counterfeits, obey Scripture concretely, live in accountable community, disciple others in their own networks, and form local churches that continue without external control.

### The governing formula

> **The message is fixed. The bridge is local. The learning pathway is adaptive.**

The *fixed message* is the apostolic Gospel — it is never negotiated. The *local bridge* is the culturally meaningful entry point that helps a particular learner begin to understand. The *adaptive pathway* is the structured process that moves a learner from human experience into the biblical narrative, then into discovery, retelling, obedience, reflection, clarification, discipleship, and multiplication. This formula governs everything that follows, and it is the only sentence in this document worth memorizing whole.

# 2. The Gospel Being Proclaimed

No method matters if the message is wrong. So the Gospel is defined first, from Scripture, before any technique is designed. Everything in Part Two is in service of *this* and is answerable to it.

### Definition

The Gospel is the good news that the holy Creator God has acted in history to reconcile sinful humanity and renew creation through the person and work of Jesus Christ — his incarnation, obedient life, substitutionary death, burial, bodily resurrection, ascension, present reign, and promised return. This salvation is given by grace, received through faith, accompanied by repentance, and expressed in a life of discipleship, holiness, love, and obedience under the reign of God.

The Gospel is therefore *not* a self-help philosophy, a moral-improvement system, a political program, a prosperity promise, a cultural-identity marker, a therapeutic tool, or a recruitment pitch for an institution. It is an announcement about what God has done in Christ, what it means for everyone, and how every person is summoned to respond.

This plan defines a common core of proclamation — centered on Christ, the cross, the resurrection, grace, faith, repentance, and discipleship — and stays at that core deliberately. Confessional questions specific to particular traditions are for the churches and authorities responsible for them to settle; they must not be allowed to displace the first announcement of the Gospel with a denominational dispute. The plan is therefore transconfessional at its core without pretending to a total neutrality it does not have: it proclaims the apostolic Gospel and leaves to the local church, under Scripture and its own tradition, the questions that belong there.

### The apostolic core: events and meanings

The Gospel holds together historical *events* and their theological *meaning*. Strip out the events and it becomes mythology; strip out the meaning and it becomes mere chronicle. Both are necessary.

**The events:** Christ came according to the Scriptures; lived in perfect obedience; died for our sins; was buried; rose bodily; appeared to witnesses; ascended and reigns as Lord; and will return to judge and restore.

**The meanings:** God is holy, sovereign, and loving. Humanity is sinful, guilty, alienated, and unable to save itself. Christ is fully God and fully man. The cross is substitutionary, atoning, reconciling, and victorious. The resurrection vindicates Christ and inaugurates new creation. Salvation is by grace, not merit. Faith is trust in Christ and his finished work. Repentance is a real turning from self-rule to Christ's rule. Discipleship is the necessary visible fruit of saving faith. The Church is the reconciled community of the Kingdom.

### The narrative spine

The Gospel only makes full sense inside the larger story of Scripture. Present Christ without creation, fall, covenant, holiness, and Kingdom, and you will produce a distorted understanding even if every individual sentence is true. The story runs:

**Creation** — God made all things good and made humanity in his image to know him, worship him, reflect his character, steward creation, and live in covenant fellowship.
**Fall** — Humanity rebelled. Sin entered, bringing guilt, corruption, alienation, death, idolatry, injustice, and bondage. Sin is rebellion against a holy Creator, not merely psychological brokenness or social dysfunction.
**Promise** — God did not abandon his creation. Through the promise of the seed, and through Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, the prophets, and the sacrificial system, he progressively revealed his plan to redeem a people and bless the nations.
**Christ** — Jesus is the fulfillment: the Word made flesh, the Son of God, the true Israel, the promised Messiah, the final prophet, the faithful priest, the rightful King.
**Cross** — Christ died as the Lamb of God who bears sin, taking the penalty sinners deserve, satisfying divine justice, revealing divine love, defeating the powers, and opening the way to God.
**Resurrection** — Christ rose bodily, confirming his identity, vindicating his work, defeating death, inaugurating new creation, and guaranteeing the future resurrection of his people.
**Grace, faith, repentance** — Salvation is God's gift, received through faith, not earned. The response is repentance and faith: turning from self-rule, sin, and false hopes, and trusting Christ as Savior and Lord.
**New life and Kingdom** — Those who trust Christ are forgiven, justified, reconciled, adopted, indwelt by the Spirit, and joined to the Church, now living under God's reign.
**New creation** — The final hope is not escape from creation but its renewal. Christ will return, judge evil, raise the dead, and dwell with his people in the fullness of his Kingdom.

### What cannot be removed without breaking the Gospel

Each of these elements does specific work. Remove it and a predictable counterfeit rushes in to fill the gap — which is precisely why counterfeits are so recognizable.

| Element | Its function | If removed, the distortion becomes |
|---|---|---|
| God as holy Creator | Grounds authority, worship, moral order | Therapeutic spirituality |
| Humanity as image-bearer | Grounds dignity, vocation, responsibility | Materialism or tribalism |
| Sin as rebellion | Explains guilt, judgment, the need for grace | Self-help moralism |
| Divine judgment | Shows the seriousness of evil | Sentimental universalism |
| Incarnation | Shows God himself came to save | Moral-teacher religion |
| Substitutionary cross | Shows how sinners are reconciled | Example-only Christianity |
| Bodily resurrection | Confirms victory over death | Symbolic spirituality |
| Grace | Excludes human merit | Legalism |
| Faith | Receives Christ personally | Ritualism or institutionalism |
| Repentance | Turns from self-rule to God | Cheap grace |
| Lordship of Christ | Defines allegiance | Consumer religion |
| Holy Spirit | Empowers transformation and witness | Human technique |
| Church | Embodies reconciled community | Private individualism |
| Kingdom | Sets salvation in God's cosmic reign | Narrow personal therapy |
| New creation | Gives final hope | Escapist religion |

This single table is the doctrinal backbone of the whole plan. Every tool in Part Two is ultimately a device for protecting one or more of these rows.

# 3. Why Understanding Fails

If the goal is comprehension, we must be honest about why comprehension breaks down. No one hears the Gospel from a neutral position. Every listener decodes new words through an existing worldview, and the same phrase can mean opposite things to different hearers.

- *"Jesus saves"* may be heard by an animist as "Jesus protects me from spirits."
- *"Jesus blesses"* may be heard through prosperity theology as "Jesus will make me rich."
- *"God loves you"* may be heard through therapeutic individualism as "God exists for my happiness."
- *"Repent"* may be heard by a survivor of religious abuse as "submit again to manipulation."
- *"Faith"* may be heard by a moralist as "try harder and be nicer."

Christian language, absorbed into a non-Christian framework, produces a confident convert to the wrong religion. The recurring causes:

**Biblical illiteracy.** Where the whole story is unknown, isolated verses become raw material for distortion — slogans detachable from their covenantal and theological context.

**Cultural assumptions.** Every culture carries prior commitments about guilt, shame, honor, spirits, ancestors, destiny, wealth, suffering, family, and authority. These silently shape interpretation.

**Religious trauma.** For hearers who associate religion with fear, control, hypocrisy, or abuse, proclamation must be joined to patience, transparency, gentleness, and credible community — or it will simply confirm the wound.

**Political capture.** When the Gospel is fused with national identity, party, ethnicity, or state power, Christ is displaced by an earthly agenda. The Kingdom of God is identical with no nation, government, party, tribe, or revolution.

**Prosperity and transaction.** Where faith is sold as a mechanism for health, wealth, or status, God becomes a means to human gain. This is religious consumerism, not Christianity.

**Emotional manipulation.** Pressure, fear, crowd dynamics, shame, urgency, and spectacle can manufacture immediate responses that bypass understanding and will. The Spirit convicts; manipulation only produces reactions.

**Institutional substitution.** People confuse salvation with membership, attendance, baptismal registration, or denominational loyalty. The Church is essential; the institution is not the Savior.

The strategic implication is blunt: **this plan must be designed as a comprehension-and-formation system, not a distribution system.** The honest measure of progress is not "How many heard?" but "What did they understand, can they retell it, are they obeying it, can they tell it from a counterfeit, and can it continue without us?"

# 4. Communicating the Gospel as Story

### The principle

The Gospel must be told as the true story of God, humanity, sin, redemption, and new creation — and *in order*, because the doctrines depend on what precedes them. The cross is unintelligible without holiness and sin. Grace is unintelligible without guilt. Resurrection is unintelligible without death. Discipleship is unintelligible without lordship. Narrative is not a concession to the unsophisticated; it is the form in which these dependencies become visible.

### Four depths of the same Gospel

The same story should be available at four scales, chosen by context, never by shortcut:

- **One minute** — for first conversations. Must still contain God, sin, Christ, cross, resurrection, grace, faith, repentance, new life. *Example: "God created us for himself, but we rebelled and are separated by sin. Jesus Christ, the Son of God, came as the promised Savior. He died for our sins, rose bodily, and now reigns as Lord. God forgives and gives new life to all who repent and trust him. Those who receive him become his disciples and live in his Kingdom."*
- **Ten minutes** — for direct conversation, expanding each movement.
- **Eight sessions** — for seekers and new groups, one movement per session.
- **Forty stories** — for oral cultures, discipleship cohorts, and church formation, told chronologically from Genesis to Christ to Church to new creation (the full set is Card 17).

### Rules of communication

Tell the story before abstracting doctrine. Define key terms rather than assuming them. Avoid slogans that false worldviews can swallow whole. Use local examples but never let them alter doctrinal meaning. Ask comprehension questions *before* asking for a response. Do not press for a decision before understanding has formed. Always connect proclamation to a concrete next step of discipleship. And always invite the hearer to retell the story in their own words.

### Retelling as the primary diagnostic

A person has not understood the Gospel merely by agreeing with Christian words. The most reliable field-level test of comprehension is **retelling**. Ask them to tell it back: Who is God here? What went wrong with humanity? Why was the cross necessary? What does the resurrection prove? What do grace, faith, and repentance mean? What changes when someone follows Christ — and how is this different from merely trying to be a good person, or from using God for success and protection?

Retelling exposes four things at once: what the learner remembers, what they omit, what they distort, and whether they are ready to pass the message on. No movement should claim it has produced understanding if it has never asked anyone to retell. The tools for this are the retelling cards (12A for any believer, 12B for facilitators and mentors) in Part Two.

# 5. Contextualization Without Compromise

### Definition and the two failures

Faithful contextualization communicates the unchanging Gospel in forms understandable within a culture, *without* letting that culture redefine the Gospel. It changes the *form* of communication, never the *substance* of the message. It fails in two opposite directions.

**Under-contextualization** dresses the Gospel in foreign categories, language, and customs that block understanding. The hearer rejects not Christ but unnecessary cultural packaging.

**Over-contextualization** alters the message to fit the host culture. The result is syncretism: Christian vocabulary carrying non-Christian meaning. Of the two, this is the more dangerous, because it looks like success.

### The non-negotiables

The following may never be altered in any context: the holiness and sovereignty of God; the reality of sin and judgment; the uniqueness and incarnation of Christ; his substitutionary death and bodily resurrection; salvation by grace through faith; the call to repentance; the authority of Scripture; the lordship of Christ over all of life; the Church as a visible community; and the hope of Christ's return and new creation. Everything else in this plan operates strictly above this floor.

### Firm in substance, flexible in form: use what God has placed within reach

A tribe in a remote part of Asia will not have the bread and wine, the buildings, the printed liturgies, or the trained clergy that an old cathedral takes for granted. This is not an obstacle to obedience; it is the ordinary condition of most of the Church across history. So the plan teaches a principle that runs through everything: **what Christ commands is binding, but the form in which it is obeyed bends to what is locally available, because the mercy of God is not rationed by circumstance.** A community with no wine may remember the Lord's death with whatever bread and cup it has; a people with no church building may gather under a tree; an oral culture with no printed Bible may carry the Word in memory and song. God does not condemn a believer for lacking what was never within reach.

But this principle cuts in two directions at once, and both must be taught together or it decays into an excuse:

- It is **not** permission to do less than one can. The flexibility is about *circumstance*, not *preference*. Where the resource is genuinely absent, God provides grace; but where the resource is at hand, it should be used, because obedience uses what God has given rather than looking for the smallest acceptable minimum. To skip what is available — baptism where there is water, gathering where there are believers, the Lord's Supper where there is bread — and to call that "freedom" is not faith; it is the self-rule the Gospel calls us to repent of.
- It is therefore **not** the permissive gospel that Section 8 condemns. "Do what you can because God is merciful" is true of the person who lacks; it is a lie when twisted into "do as little as you like because God forgives anyway." The test is honest and simple: *am I limited by what God has withheld, or am I choosing less than what God has provided?* The first is grace; the second is evasion.

The rule, stated for memory: **obedience is required; its form follows what is available; and what is available is to be used, not avoided.**

One clarification keeps this principle from overreaching, and it matters most for the sacraments, ministerial authority, and the form of the church. What is described here is a *missionary provision for need*, not a new ordinary rule of church life. Where a tradition's established order is present — ordained ministers, recognized practice for baptism and the Lord's Supper, a settled polity — that order governs, and this principle does not license setting it aside for convenience. The flexibility answers the question "what does a believer do when the normal means are genuinely out of reach?" — not "may a church discard its order because reach is inconvenient?" So the plan speaks here as an emergency missionary principle, and deliberately leaves the ordinary sacramental and ecclesial norms to each church under Scripture and its own tradition (the *self-theologizing* principle of Section 7). Naming this boundary is what lets the principle serve the persecuted and the remote without unsettling the settled church.

### To know Christ is to become responsible — and while there is life there is hope

This plan exists to produce *understanding*, not mere exposure, and understanding changes a person's standing. Before someone truly knows Christ — knows who he is, what he asks, what he forbids — their failures are partly the failures of ignorance. But once a person genuinely knows him, the situation changes: from then on, what they do against him is something they have *chosen*, not something they failed to understand. Real knowledge brings real responsibility. This is not a threat the facilitator holds over people; it is simply the weight that comes with the gift, and it is the reason the plan refuses to manufacture shallow "decisions" that were never real understanding in the first place. A person should not be pressed to a commitment they do not yet comprehend, precisely because comprehension is what makes the commitment binding.

And yet the same truth carries hope rather than despair. While a person is alive, no sin and no failure — not even a deliberate denial of Christ after knowing him — is beyond the reach of God's forgiveness, *if* the person genuinely repents: not a calculated, last-minute insurance policy, but a real turning of the heart that grieves the wrong and asks God for mercy. A repentance that is true saves; a repentance that is merely performed or hedged is not repentance at all. The facilitator must hold both edges of this honestly. The hope is real, so no one should be told they are beyond saving while they still draw breath. But the call is *now* — "today, if you hear his voice" — because no one is promised a lucid final moment in which to settle the account, and treating repentance as something safely deferred is itself a refusal of the urgency the Gospel carries.

> **A note on where Christians differ.** Exactly how deliberate apostasy after genuine knowledge relates to final salvation is one of the points on which faithful Christian traditions have long disagreed — the texts in Hebrews 6 and 10 are read differently by Catholic, Orthodox, Reformed, and Arminian readers, among others. This plan deliberately does not settle that dispute. It teaches what they hold in common: that knowledge brings responsibility, that genuine repentance is always met by mercy while there is life, and that the call is urgent and not to be postponed. Local churches will work out the finer question under Scripture and within their own tradition (the *self-theologizing* principle of Section 7). Facilitators should present the shared ground with confidence and name the disputed edge as disputed, rather than teaching a private certainty as though it were settled.

### The process

Contextualization is not a clever translation move; it is a sequence of disciplined work. **Enter humbly**, as a learner rather than a cultural conqueror. **Map the worldview**: what is ultimate here, what is the human problem, what counts as salvation, how are guilt and shame and fear handled, who has authority to interpret truth. **Identify bridge concepts** — every culture holds partial echoes of biblical truth: sacrifice, covenant, purity, exile, honor, mediator, king, adoption, deliverance. **Identify the distortion risk** attached to each bridge. **Let the biblical narrative correct the local meaning** rather than pouring Christianity into local concepts unchanged. And finally, **hand the interpreting to local believers** as soon as there are any; outsiders may guide but must not permanently own the interpretation. The goal is local maturity under Scripture.

A practical caution about the very first step. In a genuinely pioneer field there are no mature local believers yet, so it is a mistake to make their existence a precondition for the first proclamation — that is a catch-22 that would stop the work before it starts. What is required first is not a mature Christian but a trustworthy *local language-and-culture partner*: a neutral cultural informant who can tell the worker what words already mean here. Local believers take over the interpreting as they emerge; until then, the partner guards against the crudest semantic errors.

### Map concepts, not just words

The deepest contextualization error is to translate words while leaving conceptual worlds unmapped. Terms like *sin*, *grace*, *spirit*, *sacrifice*, *salvation*, *blessing*, *kingdom*, and *repentance* do not carry identical meaning across cultures, and they must be examined inside the receiving community before being used as central teaching terms.

| Term | Likely local distortion | Required clarification |
|---|---|---|
| Sin | taboo violation, loss of face, spirit offense | rebellion against the holy Creator |
| Grace | patronage, indulgence, magical favor, self-acceptance | God's unearned saving action in Christ |
| Spirit | ancestor, ghost, impersonal force or energy | the Holy Spirit is personal, sovereign God |
| Sacrifice | bribe to spirits, ritual transaction | Christ's once-for-all atoning death |
| Salvation | protection, prosperity, social acceptance, escape | reconciliation with God through Christ |
| Blessing | wealth, fertility, status, comfort | life under God's favor and lordship |
| Kingdom | political control, tribe, empire, nationalism | God's redemptive reign in Christ |
| Repentance | shame performance, ritual penance, behavior management | turning from self-rule and sin to Christ |

For every central term the facilitator asks: *What does this word already mean here? What false meaning might it carry? What biblical story corrects and completes it?*

### A minimal oral glossary before ignition

The principle above becomes a concrete first step in a new field. Before any term is used publicly, the handful of words the Gospel cannot do without — God, sin, sacrifice, spirit, grace, salvation, repentance — should be tested for what they already mean locally, because a contaminated word will quietly carry a false gospel no matter how true the sentences around it are. This does not require a full institutional translation or heavy infrastructure; it requires a short, spoken check with the language partner and, where possible, a mentor: *what does this word mean here, what false meaning might it smuggle in, and what biblical story will correct it?* The roles are distinct and should stay distinct: the mentor validates doctrine, the local partner validates cultural meaning, and a confessional authority reviews where one is available. The anchors in Card 18 and the regional profiles in Card 19 may not be used in a pioneer field until this minimal oral glossary has been done.

### Guilt, shame, and fear are entry points, not different Gospels

Cultures tend to emphasize one of three moral frames: guilt/innocence, shame/honor, or fear/power. These are not three Gospels. They are three doors into the one Gospel, and every culture eventually needs the whole of it. A faithful teacher may begin where a culture feels its wound most sharply, but must lead the learner to the full biblical diagnosis and the full biblical cure.

| Dominant frame | Felt problem | Common distortion | Entry point | Gospel completion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guilt / innocence | guilt before the law | legalism or mere acquittal | debt, court, judgment | justification through Christ |
| Shame / honor | exclusion, disgrace, lost face | acceptance without repentance | family, adoption, robe, name | restored honor through Christ's shame-bearing cross |
| Fear / power | spirits, danger, bondage | Jesus as a stronger power-tool | deliverance, kingdom, victory | freedom from the powers by cross and resurrection |

# 6. The Teaching Method: CAAGP-R

Sections 1–5 establish *what* must be communicated and *why* understanding fails. This section gives the *how*. It is the heart of the plan's pedagogy, and it is stated in full here; later sections refer back to it rather than restating it.

### What it is

**Creation-Anchored Adaptive Gospel Pedagogy, Revised (CAAGP-R)** is a teaching model that begins with a concrete, culturally real experience; uses diagnostic questions to surface existing assumptions and misconceptions; introduces the biblical narrative; guides discovery; requires retelling; invites bounded obedience framed as diagnostic; leads reflection in community; clarifies the Gospel explicitly; and moves the learner toward repentance, faith, baptism, church, and disciple-making.

The sequence:

```
ANCHOR → QUESTION → STORY → DISCOVER → RETELL → DO → REFLECT → TOGETHER → CLARIFY → DISCIPLE
```

| Stage | Purpose | Primary risk | Required safeguard |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Anchor | Begin with a concrete created reality or human experience | Assuming the anchor is culturally neutral | Local meaning check (§11) |
| 2 Question | Surface the deeper human condition | Superficial answers | Patient listening, follow-up |
| 3 Story | Enter the biblical narrative | Fragmented proof-texting | Chronological context |
| 4 Discover | Guide active inquiry | Unguided error formation | Facilitator guidance |
| 5 Retell | Test comprehension, strengthen memory | Omission or distortion | Retelling assessment (Card 12B) |
| 6 Do | Invite one bounded act of obedience | Moralism | Frame as diagnostic, not salvific |
| 7 Reflect | Examine what the act revealed | Pragmatism or pride | Heart diagnosis under Scripture |
| 8 Together | Discern in community | Subjective interpretation | Scripture-governed correction |
| 9 Clarify | State the Gospel explicitly | Works-righteousness, syncretism | Christ, cross, grace, faith, repentance |
| 10 Disciple | Move into continuing formation | Isolated learning | Baptism, church, mentoring, mission |

### Why this shape — and the honest limits of the evidence

The method is built to respect how people actually learn: working memory is limited, so concepts are sequenced and chunked; abstract doctrine is anchored to concrete experience; narrative is more memorable than disconnected propositions; retelling strengthens retention; and spaced repetition beats single exposure. These principles are well-supported in the general learning-sciences literature (cognitive load, dual coding, the testing effect, spaced practice).

What is *not* yet established is that this particular sequence, applied to *Gospel* comprehension across *these specific cultures*, outperforms simpler approaches such as plain Discovery Bible Study or straightforward propositional presentation. That is a reasonable hypothesis, not a demonstrated result. Section 9 lists the questions that must be tested before the plan claims more than it has earned. Treat CAAGP-R as a disciplined, falsifiable proposal — adopted because it is coherent and cautious, not because its superiority has been proven.

### The anchor opens the door; Scripture governs the meaning

Created realities — water, breath, light, hunger, debt, family, exile, homecoming, seed, sacrifice, justice, mercy, birth, death — are powerful entry points because they are embodied and familiar. But none is culturally neutral. Wind may mean weather, breath, spirit, energy, danger, or omen. Family may carry love or abuse, belonging or clan coercion. So the plan distinguishes *near-universal experience* (nearly everyone knows water) from *local meaning* (not everyone interprets water the same way). Before any anchor is used, the facilitator runs a meaning check (Card 11): what does this mean here, is it tied to local religion or taboo, and what false conclusion might a hearer draw? A bank of tested anchors, each with its bridge, its risk, and its required clarification, is provided as Card 18.

### The obedience experiment: diagnostic, never salvific

This is the most easily misunderstood element of the method, so it is stated plainly. The obedience experiment does **not** earn grace, prove the Gospel pragmatically, or replace repentance and faith. It is a mirror. It invites the learner to attempt one concrete act of Christ's teaching and then reflect on what the attempt reveals.

> We do not obey in order to make God save us. We attempt obedience in order to see the truth about God, the truth about ourselves, and our need for Christ. Obedience is the fruit of grace and a mirror that exposes the heart — never the root of salvation.

If a learner tries to forgive and fails, the failure exposes the heart's bondage. If they succeed outwardly, reflection may expose pride or a wish to control outcomes. Either way, the experiment prepares the learner to see why Christ is necessary.

This is, in classical terms, a pedagogical use of God's commands — what the Reformers called the *usus elenchticus*, the law functioning as a mirror that drives the sinner to Christ. Naming that lineage matters for two reasons. It anchors the practice in a recognized theological tradition rather than presenting it as a novel technique. And it sets a clear boundary: the moment obedience is heard as *contributing* to acceptance with God, the practice has failed and must be corrected at once.

A word about what the experiment is and is not. What distinguishes CAAGP-R from plain DBS is not this single element but a triad of protections DBS lacks: the culturally diagnosed anchor, the formally scored retelling, and the double Gospel clarification (a guardrail before obedience and a full clarification after). The obedience experiment is a fourth thing layered on top — and it does work the others cannot. Retelling is a *cognitive* test: it shows whether the learner understood. The experiment is an *affective* one: trying to forgive a real enemy and failing produces a felt experience of one's own inability that no amount of accurate retelling reproduces. That is its unique value, and losing it is a real loss, not a neutral simplification. So the rule is this: the experiment is *near-central*, and removing it is an exception to be regretted, not a default to reach for comfortably. Where a facilitator genuinely cannot hold the diagnostic framing, drop the experiment rather than risk turning it into moralism — but treat that as a gap to be closed by training (Card 21b), not as the normal way to run the method. Even without it, the triad still protects the group; the method does not collapse into "DBS with a metaphor." But it is sharper with the experiment than without, and the training protocol exists precisely so the experiment rarely has to be removed.

### The facilitator

The facilitator is neither an authoritarian lecturer nor a passive moderator, but an active guide, theological clarifier, cultural listener, and guardian of meaning. The role steers between two errors — the monologue that treats learners as empty vessels, and the unguided discovery that leaves novices to invent doctrine. The method is **guided discovery with explicit Gospel clarification**. The facilitator listens before teaching, learns the local meaning of key terms, asks diagnostic questions, corrects serious distortion gently and biblically, keeps Christ and the cross central, and moves people toward real community.

# 7. From Group to Movement: Discipleship, Church, and Multiplication

### Evangelism and discipleship are one motion

Jesus commanded the making of disciples, not the collecting of decisions. Evangelism is not finished when someone hears, reacts, or prays a prayer; it is rightly ordered only when it opens into learning, obedience, community, worship, witness, and perseverance:

```
Witness → Understanding → Repentance & Faith → Baptism → Obedience → Community → Formation → Leadership → Multiplication
```

### Discovery Bible Study as the reproducible engine

Discovery Bible Study (DBS) is the recommended base method because it is simple, Scripture-centered, obedience-oriented, relational, and easy for ordinary believers to reproduce — in homes, workplaces, schools, villages, prisons, refugee settings, marketplaces, or online. Its standard flow appears as a standalone card (Card 13).

But DBS has a known weakness: among biblical novices, *unguided* discovery can drift into error or settle into moralism. CAAGP-R exists precisely to supply what DBS lacks — cultural diagnosis, cognitive scaffolding, anchor discipline, retelling assessment, and explicit doctrinal clarification. The two are layered, not chosen between:

```
Anchor + Question → Story → DBS questions → Retelling → Preliminary guardrail
→ Obedience experiment → Reflection & discernment → Final clarification → Sharing
```

DBS supplies simplicity and multiplication; CAAGP-R supplies protection and depth. Use DBS alone where a group already has solid biblical categories and mature facilitation. Use the full CAAGP-R where the group is biblically novice, oral, low-literacy, cross-cultural, trauma-affected, or heavily exposed to false gospels.

### Mentoring is not optional

A DBS group without mentoring drifts. Every facilitator must be tied to a mentor or coaching circle covering prayer, holiness, doctrinal correction, conflict resolution, leadership character, family integrity, protection from false teaching, and church formation. This single requirement prevents most of the failure modes in Section 8.

### From group to church

A discovery group is not automatically a church. It becomes the seed of one when believers are baptized and gather under Christ's lordship with the marks of a church: confession of Christ as Lord; submission to Scripture; baptized believers; regular gathering; prayer and worship; the apostles' teaching; the Lord's Supper; mutual care and accountability; qualified local leadership; discipline and restoration; service to the poor; and Gospel witness. A church may be structurally simple — meeting in a home or under a tree — without being doctrinally thin.

Leaders should emerge from within the body, recognized by character, faithfulness, teachability, humility, family integrity, doctrinal soundness, and the ability to shepherd — *not* by wealth, education, social rank, charisma, foreign approval, or speaking ability. The real test is not whether a person can attract followers but whether they can faithfully serve Christ's people under Scripture.

### Multiplication and non-dependence

Every group carries multiplication DNA from the start, trained to ask who else needs this, who is open, and whom they could mentor to start a new group. A group that only receives is a classroom; a group that hears, obeys, and shares is a seed.

For this to last, the work must be able to reproduce *without* foreign money, centralized control, celebrity leaders, expensive buildings, professional clergy, proprietary technology, or political protection. The target is the four-self church: **self-supporting** (local responsibility to capacity), **self-governing** (local leaders under Scripture, not permanent foreign control), **self-propagating** (reproducing into nearby networks), and **self-theologizing** (applying Scripture faithfully to local questions). Outside donors must never control local doctrine or leadership, conversion must never be made to look financially advantageous, and aid must never be a condition for hearing or accepting the Gospel.

External funds are permitted, but "catalytic infrastructure" is a phrase that has hidden a great deal of permanent dependency in the history of missions, so the plan refuses to leave the boundary vague. The line is between *non-recurring* and *recurring* cost. External money may pay for one-time, catalytic things — translating Scripture into a new language, the first generation's training, initial materials, emergency relief — because once done they are done and the local work is not left leaning on them. External money may **not** fund any recurring cost whose removal would stop the movement: salaries that keep mentoring alive, ongoing platform operation, the running expenses of a local church. The one-sentence test: *if the outside money stopped today, would the movement keep multiplying?* If yes, it was a spark. If no, it was dependency wearing the word "catalytic," and it must be corrected now, not later. Every external injection therefore carries a dated withdrawal plan from the day it begins; money with no end date is operating dependency by another name. This is also why the volunteer force (above) is a sending-and-training cost, not an operating one: it lights the fire and steps back, and if it ever becomes the thing the local fire depends on to keep burning, it has failed the same test.

### Technology as servant, not foundation

Technology legitimately serves Bible distribution, audio Scripture for oral learners, story recordings, translation, secure communication, leader training, remote mentoring, and crisis response. It becomes dangerous the moment it replaces local discipleship, centralizes authority in a platform, turns the movement into a brand, manufactures celebrity teachers, measures success by views, exposes vulnerable believers to surveillance, or distributes shallow content faster than communities can disciple. In sensitive contexts the caution goes further than "protect sensitive data": technology must not capture the *participants* at all — no recording of their voices, faces, images, or locations, and no accumulation of the metadata (who contacts whom, when, how often, by what route) that can expose a network even when no content is stored. The design rules are simple: build for low bandwidth, prioritize audio and offline access, translate locally, avoid single-platform dependency, protect sensitive data, capture nothing that identifies a vulnerable person, and ensure every digital tool leads *back* toward Scripture, local relationship, obedience, and community. Used badly, technology simply repeats the fallacy of exposure at digital scale — the very error this whole plan exists to escape.

### A worldwide volunteer force

This plan does not wait for people to stumble onto the Gospel; it carries the Gospel to them — through patient dialogue and teaching, never through coercion, pressure, or any echo of conquest. Its natural carrier is a worldwide body of volunteers: ordinary believers from every nation who take up the plan, are trained in it, and go to their own neighbors and to the unreached, telling the story, hearing it told back, and forming communities that can stand on their own.

The three-layer field kit (Part Two) is already the shape of such a force. Anyone can begin at Layer 1; some are trained as facilitators at Layer 2; a few mature into regional mentors at Layer 3. A volunteer movement built on this spine would commission people not by wealth, credential, or platform, but by character and tested faithfulness, and would send them out under mentoring rather than alone. Crucially, it must carry the same non-dependence the plan requires everywhere else: volunteers serve, train, and then *withdraw control* as local believers mature, so that the work belongs to the people who receive it and not to the senders. A global force that planted permanent dependency would betray the very architecture of Section 7.

Two guardrails belong on this vision from the start. First, **proactive does not mean coercive.** The Great Commission sends disciples to *go* — but the going is invitation, service, and teaching, not the manufactured urgency, manipulation, or social pressure forbidden throughout this plan (Sections 3 and 8, Card 16). The aim is comprehension freely embraced, not decisions extracted. Second, **the force is subsidiary to the local church; it does not become a brand above it.** Stated plainly so it cannot drift: the force *serves* local churches, it does not replace them; it *trains* people, it does not capture leadership; it *sends*, but it does not govern permanently. Its identity is the Gospel and the local body, not an organization, a personality, or a flag — and the moment it starts behaving as an authority over the churches rather than a servant to them, it has become the centralized power this plan exists to avoid. Building such a force is the work of implementation — recruitment, training, sending, security, and care — rather than of this document; the plan supplies the method the force would carry.

### The cost and freedom of the volunteer

A force like this must be built on truth, not on romance. Two things govern how anyone is ever sent, and they pull in different directions, so both are stated plainly.

**No false promise of safety — and no coerced courage.** In hard, hostile, or unstable places there may be no guarantee of protection. The state may not protect; local authorities may be indifferent, corrupt, or hostile; international bodies may not intervene; churches and partners may have goodwill but no real capacity to shelter, feed, defend, ransom, or evacuate the people they send. This has to be said without decoration: following this plan in places of risk can cost far more than time, comfort, or reputation — it can cost freedom, health, livelihood, family, home, and life itself. The New Testament never presents witness as a cost-free activity; the apostles announced Christ without human guarantees of shelter, food, legal protection, or survival. A plan that recruits for that risk while hiding it would be lying by omission — the very dishonesty this document condemns. So the risk is named honestly. But naming it is not the same as selling it: suffering is never marketed as automatic proof of faithfulness, the possibility of martyrdom is never used to pressure the immature, the guilty, the grieving, or the impulsive, and no one is pushed toward danger by shame, comparison, or collective enthusiasm. Not every Christian is called to every place. Refusing a dangerous mission is not cowardice; prudence is not a lack of faith; and courage is not recklessness. Whoever does go should go with clear understanding, prayer, counsel, preparation, pastoral oversight, and a real freedom to say no. The governing rule: **no hidden cost, no coerced courage, no false promise of rescue** — and the absence of a guarantee never cancels the sender's duty to reduce avoidable risk, prepare people honestly, weigh maturity, protect the vulnerable, and refuse to send those who should not be sent. "We cannot guarantee protection" must never come to mean "we bear no responsibility."

**No quota, no pressure, no abandonment.** This plan was born against the idolatry of numbers; it would betray itself if it then pressed its volunteers to produce them. A volunteer is not an employee of a campaign or an agent of institutional expansion, and must never be told to log so many hours, reach so many people, start so many groups, or report so many decisions. The moment numbers become the target, comprehension is sacrificed, pressure rises, and reaction gets mistaken for fruit — the exact failure the whole plan exists to prevent. So: **the volunteer's worth is never measured by people approached, hours worked, groups begun, decisions recorded, or reports filed; it is measured by faithfulness to Christ.** The volunteer does not answer for results that only God can produce; they answer for fidelity, character, prudence, and truth. This freedom is not the absence of accountability — it is freedom *from* corporate control and *toward* mentoring, Scripture, and the local church. "Preach in your own way" means in your own language, context, gifts, maturity, and rhythm — never another gospel, never manipulation, never invented doctrine, never reckless exposure of others. Where reports exist, they serve mentoring, discernment, and care, never the surveillance of productivity or the feeding of publicity — and in contexts of risk they follow the security modes above, written only when no one is endangered. Leadership here does not command as a boss; it serves as a guardian, mentor, and pastor of faithfulness. **No volunteer is treated as a producer of results; every volunteer is treated as a responsible witness before God.** The honest, sober conversation this requires before anyone is sent is set out as a card (Card 25b).

### The Wind — a hidden name for a visible faithfulness

This volunteer body need not carry a public missionary label. In many places a name that sounds openly evangelistic attracts hostility, surveillance, false curiosity, or people who intend harm — an obvious name works almost like a target tag. So the fellowship may use a quieter internal name: **the Wind** — or, in local language, *O Vento*, *El Viento*, or whatever ordinary word that people already uses.

The name is drawn from Jesus' words to Nicodemus: the wind blows where it wishes; its sound is heard, but its origin and path are not mastered by human control (John 3). One clarification must be held with force, because the image could be misheard: the name does **not** identify the volunteers with the Holy Spirit, does not claim that they direct or contain him, and does not make secrecy a spiritual virtue. It teaches a posture, nothing more — that the work should not depend on visibility, celebrity, branding, or centralized recognition. The witness may be felt through its fruit without exposing the worker's face.

The Wind is therefore not a brand, a public organization, a recruitment slogan, or a banner of institutional power; and the name itself protects no one — only disciplined anonymity does that (Card 25c). It is an internal image for ordinary believers who carry the Gospel quietly and faithfully under the care of the local church: in safe places openly, in dangerous places discreetly, and in the most dangerous places where even the name matters less than silence. The rule is simple: **be known by the fruit, not by the face; by the Gospel, not by the brand; by faithfulness, not by publicity.**

The line that ties the volunteer's whole identity together: *the Wind does not seek to be seen, but to keep the Gospel circulating faithfully — prepared against deception, protected by anonymity, corrected by retelling, and always under Christ, Scripture, and the local church.*

# 8. Protecting the Gospel: Counterfeits and Failure Modes

Where the Gospel spreads, distortion spreads with it — and counterfeits rarely reject Christian language. They keep the words (*Jesus, grace, faith, Kingdom, salvation*) and quietly change the meanings. Every believer, not only every leader, must be trained to recognize them.

### The common counterfeits

| Counterfeit | Claim | What it denies | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prosperity | Faith guarantees wealth and health | Cross-bearing, suffering, God's sovereignty | Christ saves from sin; disciples may suffer faithfully |
| Moralistic | Good people are accepted | Sin, grace, the need for atonement | All have sinned; salvation is grace through faith |
| Legalistic | Acceptance depends on rule-keeping | The finished work of Christ | Obedience is fruit, not basis, of justification |
| Political | Our nation/party is God's Kingdom | The transcendent lordship of Christ | Christ's Kingdom is not earthly power |
| Therapeutic | God exists to make you happy | Holiness, repentance, worship | God reconciles and transforms sinners for his glory |
| Permissive | Grace means no need to change | Repentance and sanctification | Grace trains us for holiness |
| Fear-based | Convert through panic or threat | Spirit-led conviction and love | Judgment is real; manipulation is wrong |
| Institutional | Belong to us and you are safe | Christ as the only Savior | The Church witnesses to Christ; it does not replace him |
| Syncretistic | Add Jesus to existing powers | The exclusive lordship of Christ | Christ shares his glory with no idol |
| Pragmatic | It is true because it works | The historical, objective Gospel | Christ died and rose whether or not it "works" for me |

### Three quick tests

Any message, sermon, song, video, or testimony can be checked against three questions. **The Christological test:** does it center on the person and finished work of Christ — or has the center drifted to money, politics, healing, identity, or institution? **The grace test:** is salvation presented as God's unearned gift received through faith — or made to depend on merit, performance, giving, ethnicity, ritual, or loyalty? **The sanctification test:** does it call people to repentance, holiness, and the fruit of the Spirit — or excuse sin and promise comfort without transformation?

### Discernment before exposure: know the methods of deception without imitating them

A volunteer should not be sent into the field naive. In many places the Gospel has not merely gone unheard; it has been fragmented, commercialized, politicized, syncretized, or used to control people. The worker will meet hearers already shaped by false promises, manipulative religion, fear, abusive authority, or distorted Christian language. So preparation must include discernment *before* exposure — learning how deception works before meeting it from a position of vulnerability.

This does not mean becoming fascinated with evil, studying deception for its own sake, or learning manipulative methods in order to use them. The worker studies the patterns of false teaching as a shepherd learns the tracks of a predator: not to imitate the threat, but to recognize it before it reaches the flock. Concretely, the worker learns how false teaching commonly operates — how it changes the meaning of biblical words, uses fear or shame to force compliance, promises wealth or protection in God's name, turns leaders into unquestionable authorities, confuses emotion with faith, mixes Christ with local powers, makes the institution a replacement for Christ, and makes people dependent on a personality or platform.

The knowledge is protective, never a license. The worker must never turn the enemy's tools to Christian-looking ends: no fear tactics, no emotional coercion, no rhetorical domination, no staged urgency, no spiritual intimidation, no promise of material reward (this is the same anti-manipulation floor as Card 16). The patterns are learned in order to name them, refuse them openly, and lead the hearer back to Scripture, Christ, repentance, grace, and free, understanding faith. The memory line: **the worker learns deception to recognize it, never to use it.**

### Distortions the *method* itself can introduce

Orthodox content can still be deformed by poor method. These are the failure modes specific to this plan's own approach, named so they can be watched:

| Distortion | Description | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Analogy distortion | The anchor becomes the doctrine | State explicitly where every analogy ends |
| Experience-based gospel | Accepted only because it "works" | Ground truth in Christ's historical work and Scripture |
| Obedience moralism | The experiment becomes a way to earn favor | Repeat that obedience is diagnostic and fruit, not root |
| Syncretistic anchor | A local symbol redefines God or Christ | Run the meaning check; let Scripture correct it |
| Therapeutic reduction | The Gospel shrinks to self-care | Recenter sin, cross, resurrection, repentance, lordship |
| Discovery drift | Novices build doctrine from impressions | Guided discovery plus trained clarification |

### The strongest objections — and honest answers

A plan implemented across many cultures should anticipate its critics rather than wait for them. Four serious objections deserve a hearing.

**"DBS and discovery-based methods produce shallow or heterodox movements."** This is a real, documented risk, not a hypothetical one. The plan's answer is not to deny it but to refuse *unguided* discovery among novices: every group has a trained facilitator and a mentor, every cycle ends in explicit Gospel clarification, and retelling is scored against a doctrinal standard (Card 12B). If those safeguards are absent, the criticism stands and the method should not be used.

**"The obedience experiment is moralism wearing a disguise."** The danger is genuine, and the defense is the theological one set out in Section 6: the practice functions as a use of the law as mirror, aimed at exposing inability and driving the learner to grace, not at earning it. Where a facilitator genuinely cannot hold that distinction, the experiment is dropped rather than risked — but, as Section 6 argues, that is a regretted exception closed by training, not a casual escape hatch. The experiment supplies the method's one *affective* test of the heart; the triad of anchor, scored retelling, and double clarification continues to protect the group when it is absent, but the method is sharper with it than without.

**"Beginning with felt cultural needs (shame, fear, loneliness) lets the culture set the agenda."** The entry point is not the Gospel; it is the door to the Gospel. Section 5 requires every culturally-felt frame to be carried into the *full* biblical diagnosis and cure. The risk is real only if the facilitator stops at the entry point — which the method explicitly forbids.

**"This is too sophisticated for the ordinary believers it claims to equip."** This is the central risk the structure is built to answer: ordinary believers work from Part Two's standalone cards, not from this manifesto. If a facilitator needs Section 6 in hand to run a session, the field kit has failed and should be simplified further. The complexity belongs to the trainers; the simplicity belongs to the field.

# 9. Measuring Faithfulness, and What We Still Don't Know

### What not to count as success

Attendance, views, likes, downloads, event size, raised hands, prayer cards, budget growth, building projects, and platform reach are all easy to count and all nearly worthless as measures of understanding, conversion, holiness, or perseverance. They may be tracked as secondary data. They must never define success.

### What to measure instead

Comprehension (can people accurately retell the Gospel and explain Christ's work?); discernment (can they reject counterfeits?); obedience (are concrete patterns forming?); repentance (are they turning from known sin?); character (is the fruit of the Spirit visible?); community (are they gathering, caring, confessing, persevering together?); witness (are they sharing in their own networks?); multiplication (are disciples making disciples, groups forming groups, churches planting churches?); local ownership (can the work continue without external money, control, or personality?); and perseverance (faithful over years, not weeks?). The four blunt field tests: **Can they retell it? Will they obey it? Is their character changing? Can they pass it on?**

### The pedagogical metrics — held provisionally

Because this plan makes pedagogical claims, it must measure them: time to first accurate retelling; share of learners who can retell without major distortion; the type and frequency of distortions; retention at one, three, and six months; ability to explain why obedience does not save; ability to distinguish analogy from doctrine; participation in obedience-reflection without coercion; and the share of learners who can facilitate the cycle for someone else. *A movement that cannot measure comprehension should not claim to have produced understanding.*

### The open research agenda

Intellectual honesty requires stating plainly what is not yet known. These questions should be field-tested before the plan claims more than coherence:

1. Which anchors yield the highest comprehension with the lowest syncretism, in which cultural families?
2. Does CAAGP-R actually improve retelling accuracy over plain DBS or propositional presentation?
3. How often does the obedience experiment generate moralism, and which clarifications prevent it?
4. What is the *minimum* facilitator training that preserves doctrinal fidelity? (Card 21b sets a provisional floor and competence gate; the pilot's job is to test and refine it, not to start from zero.)
5. How many sessions before a learner can retell the full narrative accurately?
6. How many oral retelling generations before significant doctrinal drift appears?
7. Which early metrics best predict long-term perseverance and multiplication?
8. Which digital tools support comprehension without producing passive exposure or dependency?

Field research should distinguish strong evidence, moderate evidence, weak evidence, and mere theological inference — and should never confuse them. No method guarantees conversion, understanding, or transformation; those belong to God. The method's only claim is to *reduce preventable misunderstanding* and support faithful proclamation. That claim is modest on purpose.

This research agenda is itself bound by the three security modes (see "How to read"). It is fully applicable only in open, **Tracking** contexts; in **Sampling** contexts it runs on anonymous, aggregate data alone; and in **Silence** contexts it is suspended entirely. From this a strict epistemological rule follows, which protects both lives and the honesty of the project: **where it is not safe to measure, we do not measure; and where we have not measured, we make no empirical claim.** A movement may not point to persecuted, unmeasured contexts as evidence that the method "works." The absence of data in a dangerous place is not a quiet success to be assumed; it is simply an absence, and must be reported as one.

### A closing word on the whole

The plan succeeds only if it stays faithful to the Gospel it proclaims. If it gains audience but loses truth, it fails. If it gains speed but loses discipleship, it fails. If it gains money but breeds dependency, it fails. If it gains influence but loses Christ at the center, it fails. The Gospel does not need to be *made* powerful; it is the power of God for salvation. The task is only to proclaim it faithfully, teach it wisely, guard it carefully, and pass it on without distortion.

# PART TWO — THE FIELD KIT

*Each tool below is self-contained. A facilitator should be able to use any one of them — printed, recorded, or memorized — without the rest of this document.*

### How the kit is layered

The tools are sorted into three layers by who needs them, so that no one has to carry more than their role requires. Each card is tagged with its layer.

- **Layer 1 — Any believer.** The irreducible core: tell the Gospel, hear it told back and signal anything missing, confusing, or doubtful, run a simple Scripture discussion, and hold the three pastoral truths that keep it honest. Cards 10, 12A, 13, and 13b. A new disciple should be able to work from these alone. (Card 12B, which classifies distortions, belongs to the facilitator and mentor.)
- **Layer 2 — Trained facilitator.** Running CAAGP-R groups, handling cultural anchors, keeping obedience diagnostic, reporting to a mentor. Cards 11, 14, 16, 17, 18, 20, 24.
- **Layer 3 — Regional mentor.** Mapping worldviews, choosing entry points across cultures, training and forming leaders, auditing movement health, watching the risk register, running the pilot, and sending volunteers honestly. Cards 15, 19, 21, 21b, 22, 23, 25, 25b, 25c, 25d, 25e.

> **A warning that governs the whole kit.** The simplicity of Layer 1 is a simplicity of *form*, not of *substance*. A believer may carry fewer pages, but no layer may drop the explicit Gospel clarification, and no believer at any layer works without a mentor above them. Compress the paper, never the protection. A method stripped of its guardrails for the sake of speed becomes exactly the shallow, drifting movement this whole plan was written to prevent.

> **The Layer 1 protection floor.** There is a real danger here that must be named: if the full CAAGP-R protections live only at Layer 2, then a Layer 1 group running plain DBS (Card 13) is exposed to exactly the moralistic and syncretistic drift that motivated this whole framework, until a trained facilitator reaches it. The plan does not pretend this away. It sets two non-negotiable minimums that travel with *every* Layer 1 group, however remote: (1) **no Layer 1 group operates without a reachable Layer 2 or 3 mentor** — in person where possible, by audio or message where not, but never truly alone; and (2) **the retelling checklist (Card 12A) is the one protection the base always carries** — every group, from the first meeting, ends by having someone retell, and anything missing, confusing, or doubtful is carried to the mentor. Note the division of labor: Layer 1 only *signals* a problem; the mentor *diagnoses and corrects* it (Card 12B). The checklist is deliberately the simplest tool in the kit precisely so that it can be the floor that even an untrained believer can hold. A Layer 1 group is a seed under a mentor's care, never an orphan left to invent doctrine.

---

> **The security floor — read before writing or recording anything.** This plan is used in prisons, refugee camps, and places of active persecution (Section 7, Card 23). In any such context, a record of who believes, who leads, who is converting whom, where they meet, and how they travel is a gift to the persecutor: one captured page, phone, or recording can expose an entire network. So a rule governs every card that records anything: **in any context of risk, record nothing that identifies a person or a place — and nothing that exposes one indirectly.** That means no names and no locations, but also no voices, faces, or images; no household maps or lists of contacts and emerging leaders; and no fixed routes or predictable meeting patterns that let a network be inferred even when no content is stored. This follows the three modes from "How to read": **Tracking** where it is safe to record openly, **Sampling** where only anonymous aggregate findings may be written, and **Silence** where nothing measurable is recorded and discernment is wholly oral and in person. The doctrinal information a mentor actually needs — what distortion appeared, whether it was corrected, whether retelling is improving, whether reproduction happened at all — is almost entirely anonymous and can usually be held safely. The relational accountability that requires identities (who shared with whom, who is ready to lead) is carried *orally*, as the persecuted Church has always done. And a hard limit on this document's own competence: operational security beyond these principles — counter-surveillance, secure communications, evasion — is a specialist field this plan does not teach; in genuinely dangerous contexts, seek trained help, and when in doubt, go to Silence. Cards 12A, 14, 15, 22, 24, 25, 25b, and 25c each carry this rule.

# 10. Card (Layer 1): The Core Gospel Narrative

*Made to be said aloud and learned by heart. Tell it in order. The short lines are for memory; the paragraph beneath holds the same Gospel if you would rather tell it in flowing speech.*

**Tell it like this:**

> God made all things good.
> God made us to know him, to worship him, and to live under his rule.
> But we rebelled.
> Sin brought guilt, corruption, separation, and death.
> God promised to rescue us.
> He prepared the rescue through Israel — the law, the sacrifices, the kings, the prophets.
> Then Jesus Christ came: the Son of God in human flesh.
> He lived without sin.
> He died on the cross for sinners.
> He rose from the dead in his body.
> Now God forgives, reconciles, and gives new life to all who repent and trust Christ.
> Whoever trusts Christ becomes his disciple, joins his people, and lives under his rule.
> And we wait for the day he returns — to judge evil and make all things new.

**The nine movements, in order:** Creation → Fall → Promise → Christ → Cross → Resurrection → Grace, Faith, Repentance → New Life & Kingdom → New Creation.

**Two rules for telling it:** never give Christ without the holiness and sin that make the cross necessary; never end without the call to turn and trust.

# 12A. Card (Layer 1): Retelling Checklist — Listen for the Nine

*The simplest tool in the kit, and the protection every group carries from its first meeting. Someone tells the story back; you only listen and flag. You do not fix — that is the mentor's work (Card 12B).*

**After someone retells, ask yourself: were all nine there?**

> Did they tell of God who made all things?
> Did they tell of sin and the fall?
> Did they tell who Christ is?
> Did they tell of the cross?
> Did they tell of the resurrection?
> Did they tell that salvation is God's gift, not something we earn?
> Did they tell of faith and repentance?
> Did they tell of the new life?
> Did they tell of the new creation?

**Then carry three things to the mentor:**

> What was **missing**? — which of the nine was left out.
> What was **confused**? — what they seemed unsure of, or tangled.
> What was **doubted**? — what they, or the group, struggled to believe.

If something feels wrong but you cannot name it, that feeling is worth reporting too. Your job is to flag, not to fix.

*(In risk contexts: hold this in your memory and tell the mentor by mouth. Write no names. Record no voices.)*

# 12B. Card (Layer 2/3): Distortion Classification and Correction

*The facilitator's and mentor's deeper version of the retelling assessment. It tests the same retelling for theological accuracy and names distortions precisely.*

**Narrative integrity — present?** Main characters · setting/situation · central conflict · divine action · resolution · sequence roughly accurate.

**Theological accuracy — present and undistorted?** God's character represented rightly · human condition described honestly · sin not minimized · grace not confused with merit · Christ connected as center · cross and resurrection preserved where relevant · repentance and faith understood · obedience not treated as salvation.

**If you hear a distortion, name it and correct it:**

| Distortion | Sounds like | Corrective response |
|---|---|---|
| Moralistic | "This teaches us to behave so God accepts us." | Re-clarify grace and our inability to self-save |
| Syncretistic | "Jesus is like our local spirit." | Return to Creator/creature distinction and Christ's lordship |
| Prosperity | "If we obey, God makes us rich." | Teach suffering, cross-bearing, true blessing |
| Institutional | "Joining the group saves us." | Clarify that Christ alone saves |
| Therapeutic | "God just wants me to feel better." | Recenter reconciliation, repentance, holiness |
| Incomplete | Cross, resurrection, sin, or grace omitted | Retell the larger biblical story |

*Retelling is a diagnostic for discipleship, not an exam to shame anyone. Security floor: record only the anonymous finding (what distortion, whether corrected), never who said it or where.*

# 13. Card (Layer 1): Discovery Bible Study Flow

*Five simple steps, the same every time, so any group can run them and any group can start their own. Ask the questions; do not preach the answers.*

**1. Open.**
> What are you thankful for this week?
> What is troubling you? How can we help?

**2. Look back.**
> What did you do with what you learned last time?
> Who did you tell it to?

**3. The Word.**
> Read the passage aloud. Read it again in another voice.
> Now tell it back in your own words.
> What does it show us about God?
> What does it show us about people?
> Is there a command to obey? An example to follow? A sin to avoid? A promise to trust?

**4. Obey.**
> If this is God's Word — what will you do this week?
> Who needs to hear it? Who can we serve?

**5. Pray.**
> Pray for obedience, for courage, and for understanding.

**Rules for the one who leads:** Do not preach. Do not answer every question yourself. Point back to the text. Let them retell and discover. Correct serious error gently, from Scripture. Keep it simple enough to copy. Always move toward obeying and sharing.

# 13b. Card (Layer 1): Three Truths to Carry — Use What You Have · Knowing Brings Responsibility · While There Is Life There Is Hope

*Three truths every believer carries, in plain words. They guard against two opposite errors: doing too little and calling it freedom, and despairing as if mercy could run out.*

**Use what you have.**
> What Christ commands is binding. *How* you obey bends to what God has placed in your reach.
> No bread and wine? Remember the Lord with the bread and cup you have.
> No building? Gather under a tree.
> No printed Bible? Carry the Word in memory and song.
> Hold these two together: *God does not blame you for what you do not have. God does call you to use what you do have.*
> So this is about what you *lack*, never what you *avoid*.
> If there is water for baptism, use it. If believers can gather, gather. If you can obey more fully, do.
> To choose less than God gave, and call it freedom, is not faith.
> The rule: *obedience is required; its form follows what is available; and what is available is to be used, not avoided.*

**Knowing Christ brings responsibility.**
> Before you truly know him, your failures are partly ignorance.
> Once you truly know who he is and what he asks, what you do against him, you have chosen.
> This is not a threat. It is the weight that comes with the gift.
> That is why we never push anyone to a "decision" they do not yet understand.

**While there is life, there is hope — so turn now.**
> No sin puts you beyond God's forgiveness while you still breathe — if you truly repent.
> True repentance is a real turning of the heart: it grieves the wrong and asks for mercy.
> It is not a last-minute bargain. A repentance only performed is not repentance.
> So no one alive is beyond saving — and no one is promised a final clear moment to fix things later.
> The call is *today*.

*(How denying Christ after knowing him bears on final salvation is a question faithful Christians answer differently. Teach the shared ground — responsibility, real repentance, urgent hope — and leave the disputed edge to the local church under Scripture.)*

# 14. Card (Layer 2): The CAAGP-R Session (60–90 minutes)

*In trauma- or attention-sensitive settings, split into shorter segments or across meetings. Note that Gospel clarification appears twice — a brief guardrail before obedience, and a full clarification after reflection. **Security floor:** in any sensitive context, the session produces no recordings and no identifiable records — no audio, video, photos, names, locations, or routes. The session is held in memory and lived in person, not captured.*

1. **Grounding (5 min).** Calm, predictable welcome; reduce social pressure; pray where appropriate.
2. **Anchor (10 min).** Introduce a locally validated concrete object or experience. Don't explain doctrine yet — let the group observe.
3. **Diagnostic questions (10 min).** E.g.: Why do people know justice yet practice injustice? Why is forgiveness so hard? Why do we long to be clean after failure?
4. **Biblical narrative (15–20 min).** Tell the story orally, in sequence. Don't reduce it to a moral lesson. Connect it to the larger arc.
5. **Guided discovery (15 min).** What happened? What does it reveal about God? About humanity? What problem is exposed? What does God provide or promise? How does this connect to Christ?
6. **Retelling (10 min).** A participant retells; the group fills gaps; the facilitator gently corrects distortion.
7. **Preliminary guardrail (3–5 min):** *"We do not obey to make God save us. We obey because his Word exposes the truth about him and about us. This is not payment for salvation; it is a way to see what the teaching reveals. Salvation is by grace through faith in Christ."*
8. **Obedience experiment (5 min).** Invite one bounded act from the text — forgive one person, confess one wrong, pray honestly, serve, give, speak truth. Restate briefly that it does not earn salvation.
9. **Reflection assignment (2 min).** Carry three questions: What happened when I tried to obey? What did I learn about myself? What do I still want to know about God, Christ, or the Gospel?
10. **Community discernment (next gathering, 10–15 min).** Hear what happened. Watch for pride, despair, moralism, fear, false assurance, syncretism.
11. **Final Gospel clarification (5–10 min).** State the Gospel explicitly — Christ, cross, resurrection, grace, faith, repentance, new life — and show how the practice revealed the need for Christ and how Christ alone answers it.
12. **Sharing & closing.** Who needs to hear this story, and how will you tell them? Close in prayer; confirm the next gathering.

# 16. Card (Layer 2): Inclusive and Trauma-Sensitive Facilitation

**Attention-sensitive learners:** predictable routines, short segments, movement, physical objects, verbal recall, clear transitions. Avoid long passive teaching; ask for short retellings and concrete actions.

**Low-literacy and dyslexic learners:** never rely on written assessment. Use oral retelling, audio, images, tactile objects, drama, and repeated storying.

**Trauma-exposed learners:** connection before content. Never manipulate emotion, fear, shame, or vulnerability. Keep a calm tone, predictable structure, and non-coercive, consent-respecting participation. If a learner becomes distressed, prioritize safety and pastoral care over finishing the lesson.

**General design:** use multiple forms of representation (oral, visual, tactile, narrative, embodied), expression (speaking, drawing, acting, singing, teaching another), and engagement (story, question, service, reflection, community). And never confuse emotional intensity with spiritual understanding.

**The anti-manipulation floor — for every setting.** Do not use trauma, fear, music, crowd atmosphere, urgency, shame, or repetition to force a response. Do not promise material outcomes for obedience. Do not frame the obedience experiment as a test that makes God perform. Do not measure faith by tears, excitement, or public compliance. Do not punish honest doubt. Do not make learners dependent on the facilitator's approval. The facilitator may invite, plead, teach, warn, and clarify — and must never manipulate, shame, bribe, or pressure. Give every learner room to ask, struggle, retell imperfectly, and return to the text.

# 19. Card (Layer 3): Regional Entry Profiles

*Starting templates only — and they are deliberately stereotyped, because no one-page profile can hold the variation within "Muslim" or "Hindu" or "Buddhist" across regions, languages, philosophies, schools, and castes. To stop a facilitator from carrying these as ready-made answers — the very imperialism Section 5 forbids — a step is **required before any profile may be used**: with local believers, name at least one specific point where this profile is wrong for this exact community. If you cannot find one, you do not yet know the community well enough to teach in it; keep listening. The profile is a hypothesis to be corrected locally, never a script. For each context below: the filters hearers bring, anchors that tend to work, anchors that tend to mislead, diagnostic questions, priority Scripture bridges, and the clarification that must not be skipped.*

### Secular urban

**Filters:** individualism, distrust of authority and institutions, therapeutic identity, moral relativism, assumed science-vs-religion conflict.
**Useful anchors:** loneliness, anxiety, identity, justice, burnout, debt, homecoming, light/darkness, fragmentation and wholeness.
**Risky anchors:** mental-health language (reduces Gospel to therapy), success language (Christ as self-optimization), justice language (collapses into pure politics).
**Diagnostic questions:** Why do people seek freedom yet end enslaved to desire, achievement, approval, or anxiety? Why do we demand justice but excuse our own? Why does success so often fail to satisfy?
**Priority bridges:** Creation, Fall, Ecclesiastes, Luke 15, John 1, John 4, John 8, Romans 1–5, Acts 17, Revelation 21.
**Required clarification:** Christ is not a therapeutic aid or lifestyle upgrade. He is the crucified and risen Lord who reconciles sinners to God and forms a new people.

### Animistic / folk religious

**Filters:** fear of spirits, ancestor obligations, ritual protection, sacred places and objects, power encounters.
**Useful anchors:** fear, protection, night, illness, bondage, deliverance, authority, shelter, cleansing.
**Risky anchors:** wind, breath, spirit, sacrifice, blood, blessing — when read as magical forces, appeasement, or tools to control the unseen.
**Diagnostic questions:** Why do people fear unseen powers? Can a human safely control the spiritual world? What kind of Lord stands above every power and cannot be manipulated by ritual?
**Priority bridges:** Creation, Exodus, Elijah and the prophets of Baal, Mark 5, Luke 8, Colossians 1–2, Hebrews 2, 1 John 3:8.
**Required clarification:** Jesus is not a stronger spirit or a protective charm. He is Creator, Lord, Savior, and King. Salvation is reconciliation with God, not magical control or ritual protection.

### Muslim

**Filters:** strong monotheism, prophetic categories, honor and shame, suspicion of Trinity and incarnation, community pressure.
**Useful anchors:** sacrifice, covenant, mercy, judgment, prayer, purity, family honor, obedience, prophet and promise.
**Risky anchors:** sonship language (if heard biologically/blasphemously), sacrifice (if reduced to ritual slaughter without substitution), obedience (if heard as the basis of acceptance).
**Diagnostic questions:** How can a holy God show mercy without ignoring justice? What sacrifice can truly deal with sin? What does it mean for God to reveal *himself*, not merely send information?
**Priority bridges:** Creation, Abraham, Genesis 22, Exodus 12, Isaiah 53, Luke 1–2, John 1, John 10, Romans 3–5, Hebrews.
**Required clarification:** Jesus is not merely a prophet. He is the eternal Son made flesh, the promised Messiah, the final sacrifice, the risen Lord, the only mediator between God and humanity.

### Hindu

**Filters:** pluralism, karma, caste/social hierarchy, many deities or manifestations, cyclical time.
**Useful anchors:** longing for release, moral burden, pollution and cleansing, caste exclusion, hunger for final rest, light/darkness, bondage and liberation (incarnation handled carefully, distinct from avatar concepts).
**Risky anchors:** avatar language (makes Christ one manifestation among many), karma language (salvation as moral balancing), cleansing (reinforces caste purity or ritualism).
**Diagnostic questions:** Can moral debt be erased by endless effort? If all is cyclical, where is final justice and restoration? Can one Savior be unique without being merely one deity among many?
**Priority bridges:** Creation, Fall, Babel, Exodus, Levitical cleansing, Isaiah 53, John 1, John 14, Acts 17, Romans 3–5, Revelation 21.
**Required clarification:** Christ is not one avatar among many. He is the unique incarnate Son, the Creator distinct from creation, the crucified and risen Lord who saves by grace, not by karmic achievement.

### Buddhist

**Filters:** suffering as the central problem, desire and attachment, non-theistic frameworks, karma and rebirth, monastic/ethical ideals.
**Useful anchors:** suffering, desire, death, compassion, burden, rest, path, thirst, impermanence.
**Risky anchors:** inner-peace language (salvation as psychological calm), path language (self-liberation), desire language (erases personal God, sin, reconciliation).
**Diagnostic questions:** Is suffering only a problem of desire, or also of alienation from the Creator? Can discipline alone extinguish guilt, death, and evil? What if the final hope is not escape from creation but its renewal?
**Priority bridges:** Creation, Fall, Psalms of lament, Matthew 11:28–30, John 4, John 11, Romans 8, 2 Corinthians 5, Revelation 21–22.
**Required clarification:** the Gospel is not inner peace or detachment. It is reconciliation with the personal Creator through Christ, bodily resurrection, and new creation.

### Nominal Christian

**Filters:** cultural Christianity, sacramental/institutional confidence without conversion, moralism, tradition without Scripture, national-Christian identity.
**Useful anchors:** new birth, lost and found, inheritance, empty ritual, family name without family life, light hidden under familiarity, debt and grace.
**Risky anchors:** church language (reinforces institutional salvation), baptism/sacrament language (detached from repentance and faith), national identity (confused with the Kingdom).
**Diagnostic questions:** Can someone know Christian words and not know Christ? What is the difference between inherited religion and new birth? What does repentance look like for someone who thinks they are already safe?
**Priority bridges:** John 3, Luke 15, Matthew 7:21–23, Romans 2–5, Galatians, Ephesians 2, Revelation 3.
**Required clarification:** church attendance, sacraments, family tradition, and national identity do not save. Salvation is by grace through faith in Christ, producing repentance and new life.

### Post-prosperity (disillusioned)

**Filters:** disillusionment after false promises, Christianity associated with money manipulation, hunger for miracles and immediate relief, distrust of pastors.
**Useful anchors:** debt, betrayal, wounds, false promises, suffering, endurance, cross-bearing, transparent stewardship, shepherd and sheep.
**Risky anchors:** seed and harvest (tied to prosperity giving), blessing (heard as wealth guarantee), healing (transactional proof of faith).
**Diagnostic questions:** What happens when religion promises blessing but exploits the vulnerable? What kind of Savior enters suffering instead of selling escape from it? How can grace be free if leaders sell it?
**Priority bridges:** Job, Psalm 73, Isaiah 53, Mark 8:34–38, John 10, Acts 3, 2 Corinthians 4, 1 Peter, James 5.
**Required clarification:** Christ crucified and risen is the center. The Gospel does not sell wealth, health, or status. God may heal and provide, but faith is not a transaction for material gain.

# 20. Card (Layer 2): The Obedience-and-Reflection Cycle

*Back to the facilitator's set: this card supports the "Do → Reflect" stages a facilitator runs.*

*A practical school of repentance — how to run the "Do → Reflect" stages without letting them slide into works-righteousness. Read alongside the guardrails in Card 14.*

```
Teaching of Christ → one bounded act of obedience → real-life attempt
→ reflection on the outcome → community discernment → Gospel clarification
→ deeper repentance, faith, and discipleship
```

**Facilitation rules.** Choose one concrete action, not a vague ideal. Use no pressure, shame, reward, or threat. State that obedience does not earn salvation. Ask what the attempt revealed about the heart. Connect the outcome to the need for Christ. Celebrate honesty over performance. Bring every conclusion back under Scripture.

**Reading the common outcomes:**

| What happened | What it reveals | Facilitator response |
|---|---|---|
| Obeyed and felt joy | a taste of God's design | clarify: fruit of grace, not merit |
| Obeyed and grew proud | self-righteousness exposed | lead toward humility and need for grace |
| Tried and failed | inability exposed | point to Christ's sufficiency |
| Refused | resistance exposed | ask gently what the refusal reveals |
| Obeyed and suffered for it | the cost of discipleship | teach cross-bearing and perseverance |

# 21. Card (Layer 3): Leader Formation — Character Before Skill

*Leaders are recognized and formed in four stages, in this order. Character is first because gifting without character multiplies harm. Each stage carries its own honest tests.*

**Stage 1 — Character.** Humility, integrity, sexual purity, financial honesty, family life, servanthood, prayer, teachability. *Tests:* Does the person serve without seeking status? Is he or she truthful? Is the household credible? Does the person receive correction? Does the person love people or use them?

**Stage 2 — Gospel and doctrine.** Biblical theology; the doctrine of God; humanity and sin; Christology; atonement; resurrection; grace and faith; repentance; the Holy Spirit; the Church; last things. *Tests:* Can the person explain the Gospel clearly, refute counterfeits biblically, and teach Scripture in context?

**Stage 3 — Shepherding.** Caring for the suffering, correcting sin gently, handling conflict, protecting the vulnerable, counseling with Scripture, practicing discipline and restoration, encouraging perseverance. *Tests:* Does the person protect or dominate? Listen before speaking? Handle conflict biblically?

**Stage 4 — Mission and multiplication.** Evangelism, DBS/CAAGP-R facilitation, training others, sending, contextualization, regional cooperation, movement health. *Tests:* Is the person multiplying ministry or centralizing it? Are new leaders forming? Is the work becoming *less* dependent on this leader over time?

# 21b. Card (Layer 3): Facilitator Training and the Competence Gate

*The plan elsewhere admits it does not yet know, from field evidence, the exact minimum training that prevents a facilitator from teaching error (Section 9). This card sets a provisional floor to be refined by the pilot (Card 25) — enough to move from "we don't know" to a defensible starting standard, so that no one facilitates alone untested.*

**Minimum training content.** Before facilitating, a Layer 2 candidate must be able to: tell the Core Gospel Narrative (Card 10) from memory; run the distortion classification (Card 12B) and name the six common distortions and their corrections; explain why the obedience experiment is diagnostic, not salvific, in their own words (Section 6); run a cultural meaning check (Card 11); and recognize the drift alarms (Card 15) and the counterfeits (Section 8), including how deception operates so as to refuse it rather than repeat it (the discernment-before-exposure principle of Section 8). This is content, not hours — a literate cohort may cover it in days, an oral one over weeks of storying. The pilot measures how much is actually enough.

**The competence gate.** Training is not certified by attendance but by demonstration. Before facilitating a group alone, the candidate must, while observed by a mentor: (1) **co-facilitate** at least one full cycle; (2) **retell** the Gospel narrative accurately without major distortion; and (3) **catch and correct** at least one distortion in someone else's retelling, on their feet. Because real distortions may not arise on schedule, the gate does not wait for them: the mentor **plants controlled distortions** — a moralistic line, a syncretistic line, a prosperity line — into a practice retelling and confirms the candidate can recognize and correct each. A candidate who cannot do all three is not yet a facilitator — they keep co-facilitating under a mentor until they can. This gate is the single strongest protection against the heterodoxy risk of Section 8.

**The standing rule.** No facilitator operates without a mentor or coaching circle above them (Section 7). Passing the gate grants the right to facilitate, never the right to facilitate alone and unaccountable. Where a candidate cannot reliably hold the obedience experiment's diagnostic framing, they facilitate without it (Section 6) until further training closes the gap — the gate names that gap rather than hiding it.

**An emergency clause for isolation.** In extreme contexts — persecution, a clandestine network, no reachable mentor — the full gate may be impossible, and waiting for it would leave people unshepherded. There, a candidate of evident character may facilitate *provisionally* on the strength of recognition by the local believers and one mentor (rather than the full process), but only with **limited authority, a short defined term, and required revalidation** once a fuller process becomes possible. This is a concession to necessity, not a normal path: it keeps a clandestine group from being paralyzed by audit, without quietly lowering the standard everywhere else.

*The failure modes most likely to sink the work, with the one mitigation that matters most for each. | Risk | What it looks like | Primary mitigation |
|---|---|---|

| Discovery drift | novices build doctrine from impressions | guided discovery, trained facilitators, retelling assessment, mentoring |
| Technology as exposure engine | shallow content spreads faster than communities can disciple | every digital path must lead back to local relationship and discipleship |
| Cognitive overload | too many concepts at once | chunk, story, anchor, repeat, retell orally |
| Trauma activation | teaching triggers fear, shame, or shutdown | grounding, predictability, no pressure, referral when needed |
| Low-literacy exclusion | written-heavy materials block learners | oral, visual, tactile, audio, non-written assessment |
| Measurement distortion | teams chase easy numbers | scorecard focused on comprehension, fruit, perseverance, ownership |
| Cultural imperialism | foreign forms treated as biblical | contextualization process; local self-theologizing |
| Burnout | leaders overextend | Sabbath rhythms, shared leadership, pastoral care |
| Fragmentation | decentralization becomes isolation | regional mentoring clusters and peer networks |
| Biometric / metadata capture | voices, faces, routes, or contact patterns expose a network even without names | record no biometrics; no fixed routes or patterns; Silence mode in high risk |
| Dangerous documentation | the act of recording becomes the threat | three security modes; default to oral and anonymous; when in doubt, record nothing |
| Mentor as single point of failure | the network collapses or is exposed if one mentor falls or is captured | distributed mentoring circles, not lone mentors; no mentor holds the whole map |
| Paralysis by audit | safeguards become so heavy the work cannot move in hard contexts | emergency clauses (Card 21b); proportional, not maximal, safeguards |
| Reckless or coerced sending | volunteers pushed into danger unprepared, by guilt, enthusiasm, or false promises of safety | the sending conversation (Card 25b); free refusal; sender's duty of care |
| Quota pressure on volunteers | numbers become targets, so reaction is forced and mistaken for fruit | no quotas; worth measured by fidelity, not output; reports serve care, not surveillance |

# 24. Card (Layer 2): Facilitator Report — by Security Mode

*Keeps mentoring honest and surfaces drift early — without becoming a map for a persecutor. Read the security floor at the head of the Field Kit first. The form follows the three modes, chosen by the danger of the context. There is no fixed reporting schedule: a regular, predictable report is itself a detectable pattern, so cadence is set by the mentor to suit the risk, not by a calendar.*

**Tracking mode (open, safe contexts).** Record the full picture, including the relational detail that helps a mentor shepherd: location, facilitator, who retold well, who shared, who is emerging as a leader — alongside all the doctrinal findings below. Use this only after honestly judging that a captured page would harm no one.

**Sampling mode (sensitive contexts — the cautious default).** Record only what is anonymous and cannot expose anyone:

- **Anchor used, and any local-meaning issue it raised:** ____________________
- **Diagnostic question used:** ____________________
- **Story / passage taught:** ____________________
- **Did retelling happen, and is it improving? (yes / partial / no):** ____________________
- **What was omitted or distorted in the retelling:** ____________________
- **What Gospel clarification was given:** ____________________
- **Obedience experiment used (if any), and what the reflection revealed:** ____________________
- **Any signs of moralism, syncretism, prosperity, fear, or manipulation:** ____________________
- **Did reproduction happen — did anyone carry the story onward? (yes / no):** ____________________
- **Doctrinal or pastoral questions for the mentor:** ____________________

*Note what is absent: no location, no names, no record of which person believed, led, or shared. That is carried orally when the mentor needs it, never written.*

**Silence mode (high-risk contexts).** Nothing is written at all. Accountability is wholly oral and in person: the facilitator tells the mentor what is happening doctrinally, face to face, and no record survives the conversation. A report that cannot be made safely is not made.

*A minimal pattern for oral accountability in Silence mode — principle, not tradecraft.* The danger here is that mentoring either stops (and the group drifts) or becomes its own detectable pattern (and the network is exposed). Three principles hold the middle without this document pretending to teach security: (1) **carry doctrine, not a directory** — the conversation is about what is being taught and what distortion is appearing, never a roll of who believes or where they meet; (2) **vary, don't schedule** — contact happens irregularly through relationships that already exist for other reasons, never on a fixed cadence or route that itself becomes a signature; and (3) **distribute, don't centralize** — no single mentor holds the whole picture of a region, so that the loss or capture of one person cannot unravel the rest. The doctrinal warning signs a mentor listens for are the ordinary ones (the drift alarms of Card 15, the counterfeits of Section 8) — heard in conversation, not scored on paper. Anything beyond this — secure communications, counter-surveillance, how to vary contact safely in a specific hostile setting — is the specialist domain the security floor defers to trained help; when in doubt, the rule is still to say less and record nothing.

# 25. Card (Layer 3): The Minimum Pilot Protocol

*Before scaling anywhere, run a small pilot — and resist the urge to measure everything. The discipline of a good pilot is measuring few things honestly rather than many things vaguely.*

**Scope.** Five to ten real facilitators, each running one group through the eight-session arc (Section 4) with the full CAAGP-R cycle (Card 14). Long enough to see a second generation begin — roughly one to two cycles.

**An ethical sequence — where to pilot, in order.** The base method (telling Bible stories, DBS, retelling) is established missionary practice, not an experiment. What is genuinely unproven is the CAAGP-R hypothesis — that this particular sequence improves comprehension over simpler methods (Section 9). It would therefore be wrong to first test an unvalidated method on the most vulnerable people. The rule: **pilot in order of rising vulnerability.** Begin where participants are stable, free, and able to consent and leave freely — willing believers and seekers in secure settings. Only after the method has shown it reduces distortion without causing harm does it move toward harder and more fragile contexts (the persecuted, the imprisoned, the traumatized, the displaced). Vulnerable communities are never the laboratory for a hypothesis; they receive the method after it has earned trust, with the trauma-sensitive and anti-manipulation floors (Card 16) fully in force. This is an ethical requirement, not a preference.

**Condition the pilot on the security mode.** A pilot generates exactly the network data a persecutor wants, so what can be measured depends on the danger of the context (see "How to read"). In **Tracking** (open, safe) contexts, run the full pilot and measure normally. In **Sampling** (sensitive) contexts, measure only through anonymous, aggregate findings — never named or located lists of who is converting whom. In **Silence** (high-risk) contexts there is no measurable pilot at all: the method may still be used, but it is not studied, and — per Section 9 — its use there is never cited as evidence that it works. In short: full pilot where open, sampled where sensitive, suspended where measuring would endanger anyone.

**Measure only three things.** Everything else is noise until these are understood.

1. **Retelling.** Can learners retell the Gospel narrative in their own words without major distortion, and how many sessions does it take them to get there? (Score with Card 12B.)
2. **Distortions.** Which specific distortions appear, how often, and which clarifications actually correct them? (Name them with the counterfeit tables in Section 8 and Card 12B.)
3. **Reproduction.** Does anyone who learned go on to tell or facilitate for someone else, without the original facilitator present?

**Then revise from real errors, not theory.** Where retelling is slow, simplify the anchor or the story. Where a distortion recurs, strengthen the clarification that targets it. Where reproduction stalls, look at facilitator training and mentoring, not at the content. The pilot's job is to falsify or refine the method (Section 9's open questions), not to confirm it.

**One rule.** A pilot that reports only attendance and enthusiasm has measured nothing this plan cares about. If you cannot report retelling, distortions, and reproduction, the pilot is not finished.

# 25b. Card (Layer 3): The Sending Conversation — Before a Volunteer Goes to a Place of Risk

*A mentor walks through this honestly with a candidate before any high-risk sending — not to frighten, but to make the choice informed and free. The tone is sober and factual: the goal is real consent, not emotional impact. No one is sent who has not had this conversation.*

**Say the truth about safety, plainly and without drama.** In some places, protection cannot be guaranteed. Authorities may not help; partners and churches may want to help but be unable to. The cost of going may be high — it can include loss of freedom, work, home, family ties, health, or life. The candidate must hear this clearly, in calm and ordinary words, before deciding. The point is that they know, not that they feel afraid; a mentor states the facts and stops there, and does not use vivid detail to move someone.

**Make refusal safe and honorable.** Make explicit that the candidate is free to say no, now or later, and that saying no is not cowardice or weak faith. Not everyone is called to every place; different gifts, seasons, and family duties are real and good reasons. A candidate who is being driven by guilt, comparison, a wish to prove something, or someone else's enthusiasm should be gently steered *away* from a high-risk sending, not toward it.

**Confirm the support that does exist — and its limits.** Walk through honestly what preparation, counsel, mentoring, and pastoral care the candidate will have, and be equally honest about what cannot be promised. "We cannot guarantee protection" never means "you are on your own as far as we are concerned": the sender's duty to reduce avoidable risk, prepare well, and refuse imprudent exposure remains in full. Operational security in genuinely dangerous contexts is a specialist matter beyond this plan (see the security floor); connect the candidate to trained help, and where safety cannot be reasonably prepared for, do not send.

**Settle the freedom from quotas in the same breath.** Confirm the candidate understands they carry no targets — no numbers to hit, no reports to produce as proof of worth. They go to witness faithfully at God's pace within their own life and conscience, accountable to a mentor and the local church for fidelity, character, prudence, and truth — never for results only God can give.

**Close under Scripture and prayer.** The decision is made before God, with the discernment of the local church, with real freedom, and without any hidden cost, coerced courage, or false promise of rescue. If those conditions are not met, the sending waits.

# 25c. Card (Layer 3): The Wind Rule — Anonymity, Fruit, and Pastoral Accountability

*Use with Card 25b before any sending into a sensitive or high-risk context. This card teaches no operational security tradecraft; it sets the moral and pastoral rule: do not expose people for publicity, reporting, fundraising, enthusiasm, or control.*

In sensitive contexts the fellowship may be spoken of internally as **the Wind** (Section 7) — ordinary enough not to function as a public banner, biblical enough to remind the worker of the right posture: quiet faithfulness, visible fruit, no celebrity, no claim of control. But **the name itself is not protection.** A symbolic name must never substitute for real anonymity, wise pastoral care, and the plan's security modes. Do not trust a word to protect what only disciplined silence can protect.

The governing rule: **public anonymity is mandatory wherever exposure could create risk; pastoral accountability is still required wherever it can be carried safely.** That is — *do not expose the worker, and do not orphan the worker.* A volunteer may remain unknown to the public, to donors, to platforms, to distant leaders, and to hostile observers; but wherever it can be done safely, the worker should not be unknown to *every* shepherd. Someone mature, trusted, and accountable to the church should know enough to pray, correct, care, and intervene.

This follows the three security modes (see "How to read" and the security floor). In **Tracking** (open) contexts, names and reports may be kept only after sober judgment that a captured record would endanger no one. In **Sampling** (sensitive) contexts, written records hold only anonymous, aggregate, non-identifying findings. In **Silence** (high-risk) contexts nothing identifiable is written at all: no names, faces, voices, photographs, locations, routes, household maps, contact lists, leader chains, conversion chains, or fixed reporting pattern that could expose a network indirectly.

No testimony, image, story, statistic, or field report may be published if it could identify or expose a volunteer, a new believer, a family, a host, a person of peace, a meeting place, or a route. Consent is necessary but not always sufficient: a zealous or grateful person may agree to exposure without grasping the danger, so leaders must refuse publicity that puts others at risk even when the story would inspire supporters. The field question is simple: *would revealing this name, face, place, relationship, route, or story increase danger to anyone?* If yes — or if uncertain — do not reveal it. The Wind is known by fruit, not by faces.

# 25d. Card (Layer 3): The Open Field Check — No Place Is "Finished"

*Use when a volunteer, facilitator, or mentor enters a place where someone may already have taught, visited, or formed a group. This is not an investigation of persons; it is a humble check of Gospel comprehension.*

**Core rule: a place already visited is not automatically a place already discipled.** A field may have received the Gospel faithfully, partially, confusedly, or falsely — and a field that understood well at first can drift later through fear, persecution, prosperity promises, syncretism, moralism, institutional control, or an unfaithful worker. So first contact is never treated as final fruit, and a visited place is never treated as a closed field. The work is not only to carry the Gospel outward but to keep it circulating faithfully.

When passing through such a field, do not begin by criticizing the earlier worker. Begin by listening, with the plan's ordinary diagnostic questions: Can someone retell the Gospel in their own words? Are the nine movements present (Card 12A)? What is missing, confused, or resisted? Has Christ remained central, the cross still necessary, the resurrection still bodily? Is salvation still by grace through faith with repentance, and obedience still treated as fruit, not basis? Can the hearers tell the true Gospel from prosperity, moralism, legalism, fear, institutionalism, politics, therapy, or syncretism?

If the Gospel is clear, strengthen what is there and give thanks — do not claim control, do not compete, do not replace faithful local work. If it is partial, clarify what is missing. If it is distorted, correct the distortion gently and biblically. If the distortion appears to have come from a worker, do **not** create accusation in the field; carry the concern to a mentor or church authority through the safest available channel, under the security mode that fits the context — in sensitive or high-risk settings recording only the doctrinal problem, never names, places, routes, or contact chains. The check is of the *comprehension*, never a judgment of the absent person.

This rule creates no quotas, no territorial competition, no suspicion, and no mechanical revisiting. The Gospel does not belong to the first messenger; the field belongs to Christ. Memory line: *first contact is not final fruit; a visited field is not a closed field.*

# 25e. Card (Layer 3): Formation Tracks — Equipment by Calling and Field

*Any believer may begin at Layer 1; no one is sent to a given field without preparation proportional to that field. These tracks are not entry requirements that filter out the ordinary believer — they are equipment gathered according to where a person is actually called. Character and the Word are the floor for everyone; the rest is added by destination. No track qualifies anyone to work alone or unaccountable (Card 21b).*

**Common formation (every volunteer, before anything else).** The Core Gospel Narrative (Card 10) told from memory; the nine movements and how to hear them in a retelling (Card 12A); the counterfeits and how deception works, to refuse it rather than repeat it (Section 8); the anti-manipulation floor (Card 16); and the volunteer's freedom and cost (Section 7) — no quota, no coerced courage, no false promise of rescue. Character comes first because gifting without character multiplies harm (Card 21).

**Facilitator track (Layer 2).** Everything in Card 21b: running the CAAGP-R cycle, the cultural meaning check, keeping obedience diagnostic, the distortion classification (Card 12B), and the competence gate. Added here, not assumed.

**Cross-cultural track (for those crossing into another people).** Worldview mapping (Card 15); the minimal oral glossary before ignition (Section 5); the regional entry profile as a hypothesis to be corrected locally (Card 19); and — where the worker does not share the people's tongue — enough of the *language*, or a trusted local language partner, to learn how the people already hear words like God, sin, sacrifice, and grace. The point of language is not eloquence but accurate meaning: a worker learns the tongue to understand how to tell the story rightly to *this* people, not to perform.

**Sensitive- and high-risk-field track.** The security floor and the three modes; the Wind Rule (Card 25c); the sending conversation (Card 25b); trauma-sensitive, non-coercive facilitation (Card 16); and honest preparation for danger, with referral to specialized security training the plan itself does not provide. Survival and security skills belong here, proportional to the field — needed by the worker going into hardship or hostility, not demanded of the believer witnessing in their own town.

**Mentor track (Layer 3).** Forming leaders by character before skill (Card 21); running the competence gate and its emergency clause (Card 21b); auditing movement health without endangering anyone (Card 22); watching the risk register (Card 23); receiving reports by security mode (Card 24); running the pilot ethically (Card 25); and holding the sending conversation (Card 25b). A mentor's authority is to guard, correct, and care — never to control money, to name leaders alone, or to centralize the network.

The principle over all the tracks: **the floor is the same for everyone — Christ, the Word, character, and freedom from manipulation — and the equipment grows with the calling, so that no one is sent unprepared and no one is barred for lacking what their field never required.**
